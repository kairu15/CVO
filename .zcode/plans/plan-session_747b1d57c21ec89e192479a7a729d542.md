> **Scope and Delimitation of the Study**
>
> This study focuses on the development of a Geo-Tagging of Livestock and Poultry Dispersal and Re-Dispersal System for the City Veterinary Office of Bayawan City, Negros Oriental. The system is designed to improve the management of beneficiary farmer records, livestock and poultry dispersal, re-dispersal, geo-tagging of household and farm locations, field monitoring, animal health records, and report generation through a centralized web-based platform with a mobile companion application. It aims to provide a more efficient, organized, and accurate way of managing the city's livestock and poultry dispersal program while reducing the challenges associated with manual, paper-based processes.
>
> The administrator of the City Veterinary Office will have access to the system's main management functions. These include managing staff user accounts and role permissions, registering and updating beneficiary farmer households with their barangay, purok, and GPS coordinates, recording and modifying dispersal, re-dispersal, pass-on, and return events, and maintaining the animal type and classification records. The administrator will also monitor the dispersal map that displays the geo-tagged locations of beneficiaries across the city's barangays, oversee technician-to-household assignments, review the Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) status of beneficiaries, examine the audit trail of system activities, manage system settings and alert thresholds, and generate reports, charts, and Excel-based exports to support decision-making and program reporting. To keep the public informed, the system will also provide a transparency dashboard that displays aggregated program statistics, such as city-wide totals, per-barangay counts, and vaccination compliance, without exposing any personal beneficiary information.
>
> The veterinarian will be able to record and manage the health records of dispersed animals, including clinical case notes and derived vaccination schedules, and monitor their health status throughout the program. The field technician, on the other hand, will be able to geo-tag the locations of beneficiary households and dispersed animals using a GPS-capable mobile application, conduct field visits with on-site GPS verification and geotagged photos, submit monitoring records, and continue working in areas with weak connectivity through an offline mode that synchronizes records once the internet connection is restored. The system will also support QR-coded ear-tag cards and an in-app QR scanner to help field personnel identify registered animals during fieldwork. These features aim to make livestock dispersal records more accessible, simplify monitoring and reporting, and strengthen the implementation of the dispersal program between the City Veterinary Office and its beneficiary farmers.
>
> However, the study is limited to beneficiary registration and geo-tagging, dispersal, re-dispersal, pass-on, and return tracking, field monitoring and visits, animal health records, in-system notifications, data analytics, and report generation. The system will not include real-time GPS tracking of individual animals, online application or registration for prospective beneficiaries, payment or financial transaction processing, automated disease diagnosis, online veterinary consultations, or other features beyond its defined scope. It will serve as a tool for organizing dispersal records, monitoring reports, and geo-tagged locations and will not replace the professional judgment of the City Veterinarian or the actual conduct and implementation of the dispersal program. The system will require a stable internet connection and compatible devices, such as laptops or desktop computers for the office staff and GPS-capable smartphones for the field technicians, although the mobile application will allow technicians to queue their submissions offline and synchronize them once connectivity is restored. Users with limited internet access, no compatible devices, or insufficient digital literacy may experience difficulties accessing and using the system. Furthermore, access to beneficiary records and system functions will be restricted according to user roles and permissions to help protect confidential information and prevent unauthorized access. Although the system will include data backup features, their effectiveness will depend on proper implementation and regular maintenance.
